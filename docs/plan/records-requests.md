# Public-records asks

Requests under New Jersey's Open Public Records Act that an agent can draft for the keeper to file when convenient. Cheap to prepare, low expectations: expect "proprietary" and "not a government record" responses. The exchange itself becomes a record on the site, as the ledger does with the district's responses.

Nothing is filed until the district's current requests are answered and the keeper says go.

| To | For | Why it might work |
|---|---|---|
| The school district, as a member of its insurance fund | The Aetna negotiated-rate file, or the fund's paid amounts by facility in aggregate | The district pays the premium; the fund's records may be the district's records |
| The township | Its employee plan's equivalents | Same |
| The county | Same | Same |
| State Health Benefits Program and School Employees' Health Benefits Program | Their carriers' rate files, or Essex paid-amount aggregates | Public plans, public money |
| Department of Health | A bulk export of the licensed ambulatory care facility list | The search tool has no export; the list is a public record |

## Timing

The table reflects what is reachable in 2026: the district and its Aetna fund are the public bodies this project already has a records relationship with, and the rate files are the ask because the published insurer files are hard to map to the plans people hold. Two things change that.

**From April 2027**, when the first files under CMS-9882-F post (see [Layer B](../layers/b-insurer-files.md)), each carrier publishes one file per named network. If the fund's plan network is identifiable, its negotiated rates are public without a request, and the rate-file ask becomes moot. The ask then shifts to what the files still do not say: which network each public plan rides on (the plan-to-network mapping that Layer B calls the hardest part of this data), and paid amounts by facility in aggregate, which no published file carries until the utilization files arrive in July 2028 and even then only as "at least one paid claim."

**As the project grows**, the list of public bodies does: a second county's district, township, and county, and the state plans for any county. The drafts should be templates with the body and plan as parameters, which is what the ledger's OPRA templates already are.

Private employer plans are not reachable this way. A volunteer's own Cigna plan, for instance, is a test document for [Layer D](../layers/d-submitted-bills.md) and a first carrier for the Layer B spike, not a records target.
